# Discover: landing-evidence

> Owner: product — what the business needs and why. No technology here: the
> design says how.

## Context

The landing page sells Specgate by its loop and its commands. It does not say
what the product changes, and it does not show the evidence the project now
has: an exam of real coding agents with and without the gate, a catalogue of
the tricks agents used against it, and the control placed outside the agent's
session that caught them. A reader who weighs whether to trust a gate with an
agent's work sees a feature list, not a reason.

## Use case

1. A reader opens the landing page.
2. The first screen shows the same work without the gate and with it, and the
   measured share of runs that met the specification in each.
3. Further down: where each control runs, inside or outside the agent's
   session; how the guard and the gate decide; the measured figures and their
   limit; the nine controls with what an agent tried against each; what comes
   next.
4. A Spanish reader reads the same on the Spanish landing page.

## Acceptance criteria

- AC-1: the landing page's first screen compares an agent without and with the gate, and gives the measured share of runs that met the specification in each, with the significance.
- AC-2: the landing page shows the trust boundary — which controls run inside the agent's session and which outside — and how the guard and the gate decide, as diagrams a screen reader can name.
- AC-3: the landing page lists the nine controls, each with what it refuses and what an agent tried in the exam; the Spanish landing page carries the same.
